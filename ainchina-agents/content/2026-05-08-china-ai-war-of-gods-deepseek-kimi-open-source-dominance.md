# China's AI "War of the Gods": DeepSeek Hits $45B, Kimi Raises $2B, and the Top 5 Open-Source Models Are All Chinese

![AI technology and digital transformation in China](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1677442136019-21780ecad995?w=1200)

**Two bombshells dropped on China's AI industry within 48 hours.**

On May 6, the Financial Times reported that China's National Integrated Circuit Industry Investment Fund — the "National Big Fund" — is in advanced talks to lead DeepSeek's first external funding round at a valuation of **$45 billion**. Two weeks earlier, the same company was being discussed at $20 billion. The valuation doubled before the deal even closed.

On May 7, Moonshot AI (Kimi) announced it had completed a **$2 billion** funding round led by Meituan Longzhu, with China Mobile and CPE participating. Post-money valuation: **$20 billion**. This marks Kimi's fourth raise in six months, bringing cumulative funding to **$3.9 billion**.

And if that weren't enough to reframe the global AI landscape, OpenRouter's Q1 2026 data revealed something unprecedented: **Chinese open-source models now account for over 60% of weekly API token calls globally**, and the top 5 open-source models in worldwide usage are all from China.

The "War of the Gods" has begun. But this time, the gods are Chinese.

## 1. DeepSeek: From $20B to $45B in Two Weeks

### 1.1 The Most Sought-After Deal in China

DeepSeek founder Liang Wenfeng never wanted external investors. For three years, the company was entirely funded by Liang's quantitative hedge fund, High-Flyer Quant, which managed over ¥100 billion ($14B) in assets at its peak. DeepSeek's strategy was simple: ignore monetization, ignore marketing, ignore fundraising. Just build the best model.

That strategy worked — perhaps too well.

DeepSeek-V3 and DeepSeek-R1, released in late 2024 and early 2025, shattered global assumptions about Chinese AI capabilities. R1, in particular, achieved near-GPT-4 performance at a reported training cost of just $5.6 million — roughly 1/50th of what OpenAI spent on comparable models. The model went viral, topping app store charts in 140 countries and becoming the fastest Chinese AI app to hit 30 million daily active users.

By early 2026, Liang faced a problem he never anticipated: his researchers were being poached. Competitors were offering 3-5x salaries. To retain talent, DeepSeek needed a valuation benchmark for employee stock options. So Liang opened the door — just a crack — to a small "symbolic" round.

What walked through that door was a stampede.

### 1.2 The Valuation Rocket

| Timeline | Valuation | Key Event |
|----------|-----------|-----------|
| April 18, 2026 | ~$10-15B (estimated) | First rumors of external fundraising |
| April 22, 2026 | $20B | Tencent + Alibaba reportedly in talks (The Information) |
| May 6, 2026 | $45B | National Big Fund in advanced negotiations (Financial Times) |
| Potential close | $45-50B | Final terms pending; multiple SOE funds + tech giants competing |

> **Note:** Valuation figures based on media reports. DeepSeek has not confirmed any fundraising officially.

The $45 billion figure is staggering in context. Consider:

- **Anthropic** (Claude) raised at a $61.5B valuation in early 2026, but with ~$1B+ in annual revenue
- **OpenAI** is valued at ~$157B, but with ~$5B+ in annualized revenue
- **DeepSeek** has essentially zero commercial revenue. Its valuation is pure strategic premium

As one Beijing-based investor told Shanghai Securities News: *"Now it's investors chasing Liang Wenfeng, waiting to see which one he picks."*

### 1.3 National Big Fund: Why This Matters

The National Integrated Circuit Industry Investment Fund ("Big Fund") was established in 2014 to build China's domestic semiconductor ecosystem. Its three phases have raised over **$80 billion** collectively, investing in SMIC, Yangtze Memory, and other strategic chipmakers.

**This would be the Big Fund's first-ever investment in a large language model company.**

What this signals:

| Dimension | Traditional Big Fund Focus | New AI Expansion |
|-----------|---------------------------|------------------|
| Strategic goal | Semiconductor self-sufficiency | AI sovereignty + supply chain security |
| Portfolio | Chip fabs, equipment, materials | Foundation models, compute infrastructure |
| Policy alignment | Made in China 2025 | AI + national security integration |
| Capital source | Ministry of Finance + SOE banks | Same, but with tech-forward mandate |

> **Source:** Public filings, Ministry of Finance reports, industry analysis

For DeepSeek, Big Fund participation means more than money. It means priority access to domestic AI chips (Huawei Ascend, etc.), regulatory cover, and implicit state endorsement. The risk, as investors note, is that accepting state capital may constrain DeepSeek's open-source philosophy and international expansion.

### 1.4 Liang Wenfeng's Control

Corporate registry filings show Liang recently increased his direct stake from 1% to 34%, with total control (direct + indirect) at approximately **84-89%** depending on calculation method. At $45B, his personal stake is worth roughly **$38 billion**.

Liang has also indicated he will participate personally in the funding round — a powerful signal of alignment with new investors.

## 3. The Global Open-Source Revolution: All Chinese

### 3.1 The OpenRouter Bombshell

OpenRouter, the leading API aggregator for LLM inference, published Q1 2026 data that shocked Western AI observers:

**Chinese open-source models now account for >60% of weekly token calls globally.**

More remarkably, the **top 5 most-used open-source models** worldwide are all Chinese:

| Rank | Model | Developer | Architecture | Key Strength |
|------|-------|-----------|-------------|--------------|
| 1 | DeepSeek-V4 | DeepSeek | MoE + NSA attention | Million-token context, coding SOTA |
| 2 | Qwen3.6-Plus | Alibaba | Dense + MoE hybrid | Agent capabilities, 1.4T daily tokens |
| 3 | Kimi K2.6 | Moonshot AI | Dense | 13-hour continuous coding |
| 4 | GLM-5.1 | Zhipu AI | Dense | 8-hour autonomous task execution |
| 5 | MiniMax-Text-4 | MiniMax | MoE | Multilingual, 200M+ user product integration |

> **Source:** OpenRouter Q1 2026 report, company technical releases

This is not a fluke. It's structural. Chinese models are winning on three axes:

1. **Cost efficiency**: DeepSeek trained R1 for ~$5.6M; comparable Western models cost $100M+
2. **Open weights**: All top Chinese models release full weights, not just API access
3. **Hardware adaptability**: Chinese models run efficiently on domestic chips (Ascend, Moore Threads), reducing dependency on NVIDIA

### 3.2 What "Top 5 Open Source" Means for Geopolitics

The implications extend beyond technology:

| Dimension | Western AI Ecosystem | Chinese AI Ecosystem |
|-----------|---------------------|----------------------|
| Foundation models | Closed (GPT-4, Claude) or expensive open (Llama) | Fully open weights, permissive licenses |
| Developer access | API-gated, rate-limited | Download and run locally |
| Global South adoption | Limited by cost + English-centric training | Multilingual by default, cost-optimized |
| Regulatory risk | EU AI Act, US export controls | Domestic compliance, growing int'l adoption |
| Talent flow | US visa-dependent | Domestic + returning overseas researchers |

> **Source:** Industry analysis, regulatory filings

When developers in India, Brazil, Southeast Asia, and Africa choose a foundation model, they're increasingly choosing Chinese ones — not out of political alignment, but because they're **free, capable, and multilingual**.

For international readers, the AI digital human trend in China is a leading indicator. The data is clear: users don't care if the host is human or digital, as long as the content is engaging and the recommendations are relevant.

Western platforms (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube) are watching closely. The technology stack is largely open-source. The playbook will migrate.

## 5. Embodied AI: The $15,800 Robot and the Talent War

### 5.1 Unitree R1: Humanoid Robotics for the Masses

In late April 2026, Unitree Technology — the Hangzhou firm famous for backflipping humanoids — released the **R1 series at ¥26,900 ($3,700)**. For context, Boston Dynamics' Spot costs $75,000. Tesla's Optimus is projected at $20,000-30,000 but isn't mass-produced yet.

The R1 is 165cm tall, with 31 degrees of freedom, capable of carrying 3kg loads and walking at 5km/h. It's aimed at developers, students, and early industrial applications. But the price point signals something profound: **China is serious about democratizing humanoid robotics**.

### 6.2 The Three Battlegrounds

**Battleground 1: Capital Intensity**

The old rule was "better algorithm beats bigger compute." DeepSeek proved that in 2024. But the new rule may be "bigger compute + better algorithm wins." With valuations at $20-45B, these companies can now afford massive GPU clusters. The game is shifting from "efficient training" to "massive-scale inference infrastructure."

**Battleground 2: Application Moats**

Foundation model capabilities are converging. The differentiator is becoming **distribution**: who can get their model in front of users at the point of need. Meituan + Kimi, Alibaba + Qwen, ByteDance + Doubao — these aren't just technology pairings, they're **channel monopolies**.

**Battleground 3: Global vs. Domestic**

DeepSeek's open-source strategy has made it a global brand. But Chinese government contracts, state bank integrations, and defense applications require domestic players. The tension between "open-source global influence" and "closed domestic revenue" will define the next 24 months.

### 7.1 For Investors

| Asset Class | Implication | Risk Level |
|-------------|-------------|------------|
| NVIDIA | China demand may shift to domestic chips (Ascend, etc.) | Medium-High |
| U.S. AI startups | Pricing pressure from Chinese open-source models | High |

| AI infrastructure (data centers, power) | China building massive domestic compute; global CapEx rising | Low-Medium |

> **Source:** Market analysis, not investment advice

### 7.2 For Developers

The practical implication is immediate: if you're building an AI application, Chinese open-source models now offer **90-95% of GPT-4 capability at 10-20% of the API cost**, with full weight downloads for on-premise deployment.

The "China discount" in AI is real, and it's growing.

### 7.3 For Policymakers

The open-source dominance creates a policy paradox for Western governments:

- **Restrict Chinese AI?** You'd be restricting the most widely adopted open-source models in the Global South.
- **Allow unrestricted use?** You'd be ceding foundational AI infrastructure to Chinese-origin technology.
- **Build competing open models?** The U.S. has Llama (Meta) and Mistral (Europe), but neither matches Chinese adoption rates.

There is no easy answer. The "War of the Gods" is as much geopolitical as it is technological.
