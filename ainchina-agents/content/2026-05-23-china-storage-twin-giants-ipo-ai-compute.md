# China's Storage Twin Giants Race to IPO: A $500B Revaluation of the AI Compute Foundation

![Semiconductor wafer manufacturing representing China's memory chip breakthrough](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1518770660439-4636190af475?w=1200)

**Two companies. One IPO filing. One IPO辅导备案. A combined valuation that could exceed $500 billion. And a single catalyst: AI's insatiable hunger for memory.**

On May 17, CXMT (长鑫科技, "Changxin Technology") updated its prospectus for a Shanghai STAR Market IPO that would raise **29.5 billion yuan ($4.1 billion)** — the second-largest STAR Market listing ever, behind only SMIC. Four days later, on May 19, its counterpart YMTC (长江存储, "Yangtze Memory Technologies") filed IPO辅导辅导备案 with the CSRC, signaling its own sprint toward a public listing.

The timing is not coincidental. Both companies just reported Q1 2026 earnings that can only be described as **explosive**:

- **CXMT**: Revenue of **50.8 billion yuan** (+719% YoY). Net profit of **24.76 billion yuan** (+1,688% YoY). The company is now earning nearly **300 million yuan per day**.
- **YMTC**: Revenue exceeded **20 billion yuan** in Q1 (+100% YoY), with global NAND flash market share surpassing **12%**.

Analysts now estimate that CXMT alone could list at **3–4 trillion yuan** ($420–560 billion), while YMTC could reach **500 billion to 1 trillion yuan** ($70–140 billion). Together, they would form a **4 trillion yuan ($560 billion)** storage behemoth — larger than Netflix, Salesforce, and AMD combined.

For global investors tracking China's AI ecosystem, this is a watershed moment. Memory is not a peripheral component; it is the **bottleneck of modern AI infrastructure**. Every LLM training run, every inference server, every GPU cluster is constrained by memory bandwidth and capacity. And for the first time, China has not just a "domestic alternative" but a **genuine pricing power player** in the global memory oligopoly.

### 1.2 What's Driving the Explosion

Three structural forces converged in Q1 2026:

**Force 1: AI-Driven Memory Supercycle**

Global DRAM contract prices rose **90–95%** in Q1 2026 versus the prior year. The driver: AI data centers are consuming memory at unprecedented scale. A single NVIDIA H100 server requires ~2TB of DDR5. Scale that to a 10,000-GPU cluster, and you need **20 petabytes** of DRAM just for the host memory — not counting the HBM on the GPUs themselves.

Samsung and SK Hynix have diverted substantial capacity to HBM3E production (for AI training), creating a supply vacuum in commodity DDR5. CXMT, which had previously been a marginal player, suddenly found itself the **only large-scale alternative** for Chinese server OEMs and cloud providers.

**Force 2: Geopolitical Premium**

On May 14, 2026, the U.S. government authorized NVIDIA to sell H200 chips to 10 designated Chinese companies. While this relaxes compute constraints, it does not relax **memory constraints**. Chinese tech giants — Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance, Huawei — are building domestic AI infrastructure. They need domestic memory suppliers for supply chain security. CXMT is the only option at scale.

**Force 3: Product Mix Upgrade**

CXMT is accelerating its transition from DDR4/LPDDR4 to **DDR5 and LPDDR5**. By end of 2025, DDR5 already accounted for 7% of output and LPDDR5 for 9%. More critically, the company is preparing **HBM3** production for 2026 Q2 — the high-margin memory that goes directly into AI accelerators. HBM carries **3–5x the ASP** of commodity DRAM.

## 2. YMTC: The NAND Dark Horse

While CXMT grabs headlines with its astronomical profit growth, YMTC is playing a quieter but equally significant game in **NAND flash** — the storage technology that powers everything from smartphones to enterprise SSDs to AI data lakes.

### 2.1 Technical Breakthrough: Xtacking 4.0

YMTC's proprietary **Xtacking** architecture is the key differentiator. While Samsung and SK Hynix build 3D NAND by stacking layers vertically, YMTC's approach uses **wafer-to-wafer bonding** — essentially building the memory array and logic circuitry on separate wafers, then bonding them together. This allows:

- **Higher I/O speed**: X4-9070 achieves **7,000+ MB/s** — competitive with Samsung's flagship
- **Higher density**: 20 Gb/mm² at 232 layers (equivalent to ~294 layers via dual-deck stacking)
- **Faster time-to-market**: Logic can be fabricated on advanced nodes while memory uses mature processes

YMTC is now the **only Chinese company** with commercial 3D NAND production, and its global market share has quietly grown to **12–13%**.

### 2.2 The IPO Trajectory

| **Stage** | IPO辅导辅导备案 filed May 19, 2026 |
| **Sponsors** | CITIC Securities + CSC (中信证券 + 中信建投) |

| **Valuation Scenarios** | Conservative: ¥300B / Neutral: ¥500B / Bull: ¥1T+ |
| **Key Catalyst** | 3rd fab coming online H2 2026; 100% domestic supply chain target |

**The geopolitical angle is even sharper for YMTC.** The company was added to the U.S. Entity List in 2022, cutting it off from advanced lithography equipment. Its response: building a **fully domestic supply chain**. By 2026, YMTC aims to have a production line using only China-sourced equipment — a feat no other leading memory maker has attempted.

### 2.3 The AI Connection: Enterprise SSDs

NAND flash may seem less "sexy" than DRAM for AI, but it is equally critical:

- **AI training datasets**: Modern LLMs are trained on petabytes of text and multimodal data. Storing and preprocessing this data requires massive SSD arrays.
- **Checkpoints**: Training runs that cost millions of dollars need frequent checkpointing to disk. Slow storage = wasted GPU time.
- **Inference caching**: KV-cache for long-context inference can spill to NVMe SSDs when GPU HBM is full.

YMTC's enterprise SSDs are already being qualified by major Chinese server OEMs. As data center buildouts accelerate, NAND demand is projected to grow **30%+ annually** through 2027.

## 3. Market Context: Why Now?

### 3.1 The Global Memory Oligopoly Is Cracking

For decades, memory was a three-player game:

| Company | Country | DRAM Share | NAND Share | AI Exposure |
|---------|---------|-----------|------------|-------------|
| **Samsung** | Korea | 34% | ~35% | HBM leader |
| **SK Hynix** | Korea | 34% | ~20% | HBM leader; NVIDIA partner |
| **Micron** | USA | 23% | ~12% | Limited HBM; US policy beneficiary |
| **CXMT** | China | 7.7% | — | DDR5 ramping; HBM incoming |
| **YMTC** | China | — | 12–13% | Xtacking architecture; domestic supply chain |

The traditional oligopoly (Samsung + SK Hynix + Micron = 90%+ of DRAM) is now facing a **structural challenge**: they are **prioritizing HBM** because the margins are irresistible. A single HBM3E stack sells for **$200–300** versus ~$20 for a DDR5 module. But HBM capacity comes at the expense of commodity DRAM and NAND.

This creates a **supply vacuum** that CXMT and YMTC are uniquely positioned to fill — especially for the Chinese market, which represents ~30% of global semiconductor consumption.

### 3.2 NVIDIA H200 Approval Changes the Equation

On May 14, 2026, the U.S. government authorized NVIDIA to sell **H200 chips** to 10 large Chinese companies. This is a significant policy shift — the H200 substantially outperforms domestic alternatives like Huawei Ascend 910B.

But more chips mean **more memory demand**. Each H200 server needs DDR5 host memory, NVMe SSDs for storage, and the chip itself uses HBM3E. With H200 now flowing into China, the memory bottleneck tightens further. Chinese distributors are expected to be the primary near-term beneficiaries, but the **structural winner is domestic memory production**.

### 3.3 A-Share Market: Ready for a Tech Revaluation

The STAR Market (科创板) was created in 2019 specifically to host China's hard-tech companies. But until recently, it was dominated by loss-making biotech and immature chip designers. CXMT and YMTC are different — they are **profitable, growing, and globally competitive**.

## 6. Why the World Should Care

For international observers, the CXMT/YMTC IPO story is not just a financial event. It is a **structural indicator** of three mega-trends:

**Trend 1: AI Infrastructure Is National Infrastructure**

China's approach to AI is not "let startups compete." It is "build the entire supply chain domestically." Memory is the final major component where China had a gap. With CXMT and YMTC reaching commercial scale, that gap is closing.

**Trend 2: The Memory Oligopoly Is Over**

For 30 years, Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron controlled memory pricing. A duopoly-plus-one is still a concentrated market. The entry of two Chinese players with state backing, captive demand, and cost advantages **fundamentally changes the competitive dynamics**.

**Trend 3: AI Economics Are Memory Economics**

The conversation about AI costs focuses on GPUs. But the real cost driver over a server's 4-year life is **memory refresh, storage expansion, and data movement**. As Chinese companies build AI infrastructure optimized for domestic memory, their **total cost of ownership** will diverge from Western competitors using Samsung/SK Hynix supply.
